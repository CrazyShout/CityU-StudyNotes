\textbf{The supplied key selects A, B, C and D.} NB estimates priors and class-conditional distributions, then derives posteriors. For binary \(Y\in\{0,1\}\), let \(p=P(Y=1\mid x)\). Then \(\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid x)=p(1-p)\) is largest at \(p=1/2\), the usual equal-cost decision boundary. Non-Gaussian NB can be nonlinear, and NB/LR can use MLE. E is false because smoothing regularizes NB. A describes the resulting posterior, not direct discriminative training.
