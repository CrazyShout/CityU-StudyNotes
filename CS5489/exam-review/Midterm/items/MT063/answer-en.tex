Draw a parabola between \ensuremath{-}1 and 1, joined smoothly to straight tails: L(0)=0, L(\ensuremath{\pm}1)=1/2 and L(\ensuremath{\pm}2)=3/2. Its residual derivative is r inside the interval and sign(r) outside. Thus extreme output residuals have bounded gradient magnitude, reducing their influence relative to squared loss. Unlike pure absolute loss, the curve is differentiable at zero and at the two joining points.
